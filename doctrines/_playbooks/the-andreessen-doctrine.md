# The Andreessen Doctrine
**Subject:** Marc Andreessen
**Created:** 2026-05-02

> The only thing that matters is getting to product/market fit.

---

## Summary

A three-layer filter for evaluating startup ideas distilled from Marc Andreessen's revealed taste: the Pmarchive guide-to-startups corpus, 'Why Software Is Eating the World' (2011), 'It's Time to Build' (2020), 'The Techno-Optimist Manifesto' (2023), the Little Tech and American Dynamism agendas, the a16z portfolio shape, his operating record at Netscape / Loudcloud-Opsware / Ning, and the long-form spoken corpus with Cowen, Fridman, Rogan, Bari Weiss and the a16z podcast through 2026. Where Thiel hunts for monopolies and concentrates a fund, Andreessen hunts for waves, technical founders, and ratable PMF — the playbook is portfolio-shaped, growth-shaped, and operator-shaped.

---

## Layer 01 · Problem-Selection Taste
*What is worth working on at all*

The filter is built around technology waves and product/market fit. Andreessen rejects the consensus that markets are saturated; the right question is which industry is being eaten by software next, and whether you are the technical founder positioned to ride that wave. The trap is whining about decline instead of building; the alpha is finding the demand a real user is already trying to satisfy with worse tools.

---

**01.01 · Software Eats Everything Filter** `Essay`

**Rule:** Every industry will eventually be reshaped by software. The only question is whether the incumbent or the insurgent does the eating. Pick a vertical where the incumbent is software-illiterate and the insurgent can credibly own the stack.

**Test:** Name the incumbent's CTO and their technical headcount as a % of revenue. If the incumbent has zero technical leadership at the top table, the vertical is eatable.

*Source: Why Software Is Eating the World, WSJ 2011*

---

**01.02 · Product/Market Fit Is the Only Thing** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Until you have product/market fit, do nothing else. After you have it, do everything else. The market pulls product out of the startup; the founder's job is to recognize the pull and feed it.

**Test:** Are users showing up faster than you can serve them, calling friends to sign up, refusing to give the product back? If you have to ask whether you have PMF, you don't.

*Source: Pmarchive: The Only Thing That Matters, 2007*

---

**01.03 · It's Time to Build** `Essay`

**Rule:** The defining problem of the West is not invention — it is deployment. Pick problems where the bottleneck is permission, will, or organizational capacity, not breakthrough science.

**Test:** Has this problem been technically solvable for 10+ years and yet remains unsolved at scale? If yes, the binding constraint is build-courage, and a founder who builds anyway captures the wave.

*Source: It's Time to Build, April 2020*

---

**01.04 · The Techno-Capital Machine** `Essay`

**Rule:** Markets and technology compound. Refuse the stagnationist, malthusian, regulator-as-solution frame. Bet on the machine, not on people protecting you from it.

**Test:** Does the thesis assume technology adoption + capital formation will continue, or that they will be paused by some coordination regime? Bet against the latter.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Techno-Capital Machine section)*

---

**01.05 · Sharks Grow or Die** `Essay`

**Rule:** Societies, like sharks, grow or die. Stagnation is not a steady state — it leads to zero-sum thinking, internal fighting, degradation, collapse. Pick problems whose success expands the pie, not ones that ration a shrinking one.

**Test:** If this company wins, does the addressable economic surface grow, or does it merely redistribute existing surface? Redistribute-only theses are stagnationist in disguise.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technology section)*

---

**01.06 · Technology Is the Only Perpetual Source of Growth** `Essay`

**Rule:** Population growth and natural resource extraction have hard ceilings; technology does not. Any thesis whose growth model depends on more bodies or more extraction is fragile. The investable thesis routes growth through productivity.

**Test:** Strip population and resource-extraction tailwinds from the model. Does the company still compound? If the answer is 'we win because more people / more land / more oil,' the thesis is not technology-shaped.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technology section)*

---

**01.07 · No Material Problem Is Unsolvable** `Essay`

**Rule:** There is no material problem — whether created by nature or by technology — that cannot be solved with more technology. Reject 'this is just an inherent limit of physics/biology/society' arguments unless the founder can name the binding equation.

**Test:** Ask the founder which physical law the problem is bounded by. If they cannot cite one, the limit is engineering, not physics — and engineering is investable.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technology section)*

---

**01.08 · Markets as Discovery Machine** `Essay`

**Rule:** The market is an exploratory, evolutionary, adaptive intelligence — Hayek's Knowledge Problem makes centralized solutions doomed. Pick problems where the answer must be discovered through millions of edge transactions, not designed by a planner.

**Test:** Does this product require a central planner to set the right answer once, or does it require a market mechanism to discover the answer continuously? Fund the second; short the first.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Markets section)*

---

**01.09 · The Nordhaus 50:1 Rule** `Essay`

**Rule:** Creators of technology capture roughly 2% of the value they generate; 98% flows to society as social surplus. Technological innovation in a market system is inherently philanthropic at a 50:1 ratio. Prestige hand-wringing about 'tech extracting value' inverts the empirics.

**Test:** Estimate the consumer surplus this product creates vs the producer surplus it captures. If the ratio is roughly Nordhaus-shaped (consumer >> producer), the company is a moral good — fund through critique.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Markets section, citing Nordhaus)*

---

**01.10 · Energy Is the Foundational Engine** `Essay`

**Rule:** Energy is upstream of everything — light, safety, warmth, compute, food, mobility. The right thesis assumes energy abundance (1000x per capita) is on-ramp, not anathema. Bet on the technologies that make energy too cheap to meter: nuclear fission, fusion, geothermal, advanced grid.

**Test:** Does this company assume energy is scarce-and-rationed, or abundant-and-cheap? Companies that bet on rationing (carbon credits as core revenue, austerity-as-product) are short the manifesto's central thesis.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Energy section)*

---

**01.11 · Nuclear Is the Silver Bullet** `Essay`

**Rule:** Nuclear fission is the existing zero-emissions energy silver bullet; fusion is the second. The Precautionary Principle suppression of nuclear is 'perhaps the most catastrophic mistake in Western society in my lifetime.' Re-opening the nuclear envelope is one of the few positive-NPV regulatory wedges available.

**Test:** In an energy-adjacent company, does the thesis route around nuclear, or does it lean into the nuclear/SMR/fusion build-out? Build-out exposure is the manifesto-aligned posture.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Energy section)*

---

**01.12 · People Are the Ultimate Resource** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per Julian Simon: more people means more creativity, more ideas, more progress. The planet is dramatically underpopulated relative to the population intelligence + energy + abundance could support. Depopulation theses are anti-growth, anti-creativity, anti-investable.

**Test:** Does this company's win-state assume more humans alive, more humans skilled, more humans productive — or does it assume a smaller, leaner, rationed humanity? Fund the first.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Abundance section, citing Julian Simon)*

---

**01.13 · Falling Prices Are the Measure of Abundance** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per Buckminster Fuller's ephemeralization (and economists' dematerialization): technology lets you do more with less, until the price of doing it approaches zero. The right thesis drives prices in its category toward zero — not protects margins through artificial scarcity.

**Test:** Over a 10-year arc, does this company drive its category's prices down by an order of magnitude? If the business model requires keeping prices high, it's a rent, not a wave.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Abundance section, citing Fuller)*

---

**01.14 · AI Deceleration Costs Lives** `Essay`

**Rule:** AI is a universal problem solver. Any deceleration of AI capability — through pause-letters, governance regimes, capability moratoria — costs lives that the AI would have saved. Deaths preventable by a forbidden AI are a form of moral debt the regulator pays in.

**Test:** Does the company accelerate the deployment of capability into life-saving domains (medicine, transportation, food, safety), or does it route through a permission-granting body? Fund the first; treat permission-routing as an existential risk to the thesis itself.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Intelligence section)*

---

**01.15 · Augmented Intelligence Compounds Wages** `Essay`

**Rule:** AI augments humans; augmentation drives marginal productivity; productivity drives wages; wages drive demand; demand drives new supply. The loop has no upper bound. Reject the 'AI takes jobs' framing as a static-pie error.

**Test:** Does the company's adoption case explicitly model the human-in-the-loop becoming more productive, or does it model human displacement? Model #1 is investable; model #2 is politically fragile and historically wrong.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Intelligence section)*

---

**01.16 · Liberal-Democracy Technological Strength** `Essay`

**Rule:** America and her allies should be technologically strong; technologically weak liberal democracies lose to autocratic rivals, making everyone worse off. National-strategic technology is not just a niche — it is a moral imperative downstream of the manifesto.

**Test:** Does the company strengthen a liberal democracy's economic, cultural, or military position, or is it neutral on the geopolitical axis? Manifesto-aligned founders pick the first deliberately.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technological Values section)*

---

**01.17 · Technology Is Universalist** `Essay`

**Rule:** Technology doesn't care about ethnicity, race, religion, national origin, gender, sexuality, political views, or any other identity axis — it is built by a virtual United Nations of talent and demands only a positive attitude and a cheap laptop. Any thesis that gates contribution by identity is anti-meritocratic and self-defeating.

**Test:** Is the founder hiring globally for talent, or filtering on identity politics? Manifesto-aligned posture is universalist meritocracy — the open society applied to recruiting.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technological Values section)*

---

**01.18 · Constrained Vision, Not Utopia** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per Sowell: the Constrained Vision takes people as they are, tests ideas empirically, and liberates people to make their own choices. Reject the Unconstrained Vision (Utopia, Communism, Expertise) that re-engineers humans to fit the plan. Build for the humans you actually have.

**Test:** Does the founder's plan require humans to behave better than they do, or does it route around human nature with mechanism design? Fund the second.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Not Utopia But Close Enough section, citing Sowell)*

---

**01.19 · Live in the Wave, Not the Idea** `a16z`

**Rule:** Ideas are cheap; waves are scarce. The right framing is 'what wave is cresting in 18-36 months that almost no one is positioned for' — not 'what idea is novel.'

**Test:** Plot the underlying enabling tech (compute, bandwidth, model size, biology cost-curve). What inflection happens in the next 36 months that makes today's impossible idea cheap? Name it or pass.

*Source: a16z thesis posts; Pmarchive: timing essays*

---

**01.20 · Find a Big Existing Market** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Better to enter a giant market with a 10x product than a small market with a 100x product. Market size dominates execution differences over a decade.

**Test:** Is the customer already paying real money for the worse alternative? If 'no, we have to educate them,' the timing is wrong or the market is imaginary.

*Source: Pmarchive: The Only Thing That Matters; The Three Things, 2007*

---

**01.21 · Demand Pre-Exists; You Find It** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** PMF is discovered, not engineered. Real demand exists in the world before the company arrives — the founder's job is to instrument the market and detect when usage stops requiring persuasion.

**Test:** Are users finding you, or are you finding them? If your sales motion is push-push-push, you have not yet found the wave — keep searching.

*Source: Pmarchive: The Only Thing That Matters, 2007*

---

**01.22 · Atoms Need Software, Not the Other Way Around** `a16z`

**Rule:** Pure-bits SaaS is overfished. The next decade's largest categories — defense, energy, biology, manufacturing, housing — are atoms problems that software can finally orchestrate end-to-end.

**Test:** Does the company own a physical artifact, regulated process, or supply chain — with software as the central nervous system? If it's pure SaaS, ask whether anyone needs another one.

*Source: American Dynamism thesis (a16z, Boyle 2022); Andreessen on Cowen 2024*

---

**01.23 · American Dynamism Wedge** `a16z`

**Rule:** Sectors where the U.S. national interest, regulator, and capital markets are aligned for re-industrialization (defense, aerospace, energy, public-safety, education, housing, supply chain) are the cleanest hunting ground for category-defining outcomes.

**Test:** Does the company serve a buyer with a national-strategic mandate (DoD, DoE, NRC, state DOTs) AND a deployment timeline that survives a political transition? If yes, the wedge is durable.

*Source: American Dynamism agenda, a16z 2022-2026*

---

**01.24 · Little Tech vs Big Tech** `a16z`

**Rule:** Frame your company as Little Tech — startups, founders, the insurgent — not as a member of the existing FAANG-shaped tech industry. Policy fights, hiring, narrative, regulator engagement all hinge on this framing.

**Test:** Does the founder pattern-match to 'Big Tech alumnus building Big Tech 2' or to 'someone the regulator does not yet have a category for'? Fund the second.

*Source: Little Tech agenda, a16z 2024*

---

**01.25 · AI Is Industrial, Not Existential** `Essay`

**Rule:** AI risk discourse is a moral panic that lobbies for regulatory capture. The real risk is concentration in 2-3 government-aligned labs. Bet on broad deployment and open weights, not on safety-institute mediation.

**Test:** Does the thesis depend on a global AI-governance regime, or does it survive — and arguably thrive — in a deploy-everywhere world? Fund only the second.

*Source: Why AI Will Save the World (a16z, June 2023); Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Intelligence section); Andreessen on Lex Fridman 2023*

---

**01.26 · Crypto as Exit Option** `a16z`

**Rule:** Crypto's load-bearing function is providing exit and programmability outside legacy rails — not 'replacing all banks tomorrow.' The investable wedge is wherever exit-option demand is highest (sovereigns, payments rails, identity, tokenization).

**Test:** Does the protocol/product solve a real demand for permissionless exit, settlement, or composability? If the only pitch is 'decentralized X' with no exit-option user, pass.

*Source: a16z crypto thesis, 2018-2024*

---

**01.27 · Bio Is Engineering** `a16z`

**Rule:** Biology is becoming an engineering discipline — cost curves on sequencing, synthesis, and protein design now resemble silicon. Bet on platforms that import software-style iteration into wetware.

**Test:** Is the company iterating in days (CRISPR/AI-protein/cell-design) rather than years (classical pharma)? If the loop time is pharma-shaped, the bet is pharma-shaped.

*Source: a16z bio thesis, 2015-2025*

---

**01.28 · Reject Decline-Managers** `Essay`

**Rule:** Any business whose thesis is 'manage the decline gracefully' is a short. The frontier is ambition, not stewardship — fund founders who refuse to accept decline as priced-in.

**Test:** When asked 'what does the world look like in 20 years if you win,' does the founder describe abundance, frontier, growth — or does the answer sound like risk-mitigation?

*Source: It's Time to Build; Techno-Optimist Manifesto*

---

**01.29 · Schlep-Heavy Is Underpriced** `a16z`

**Rule:** Problems requiring real-world coordination — permits, hardware, sales calls, regulatory dialogue — are systematically underfunded because software founders flinch from them. The schlep premium is real.

**Test:** Does the work require sustained interface with regulators, supply chains, or unsexy human channels? If yes, expect the multiple expansion when it works.

*Source: Pmarchive guide; American Dynamism essays*

---

**01.30 · Technical Founder Mandate** `a16z`

**Rule:** Invest in technical founders who can be coached into CEOs — not in business co-founders looking for a CTO. The technical founder owns the iteration loop and hires more like themselves.

**Test:** Did the founder personally write the v1 product, or are they 'looking for a technical co-founder'? Only the first is fundable.

*Source: a16z founding thesis, 2009; Pmarchive: Hiring*

---

**01.31 · Two-in-a-Box Founders** `a16z`

**Rule:** The strongest pattern is a technical founder + go-to-market founder bound by years of trust (Marc & Ben, Page & Brin, Hewlett & Packard). Solo founders can work; mismatched pairs cannot.

**Test:** Have the founders shipped something difficult together before? If the pairing was assembled at YC office hours three months ago, expect the breakup at Series A.

*Source: Marc & Ben pattern, a16z; Hard Thing About Hard Things*

---

**01.32 · Domain Obsession Beats Pedigree** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** The best founders are obsessed with the domain itself, not with being founders. Generalist founders chasing 'a startup idea' lose to domain insiders who couldn't not build the thing.

**Test:** Has the founder been thinking about this problem for 5+ years — in side projects, in personal frustration, in writing? If the obsession is younger than the company, it isn't real.

*Source: Pmarchive: How to hire; Cowen interview 2022*

---

**01.33 · Refuse the Globalization-Only Thesis** `a16z`

**Rule:** Bringing existing tech to a new geography is a real business but not a venture business. The category-defining outcomes require new tech, new behavior, or new platform — not just diffusion.

**Test:** Strip the geographic argument from the pitch. Is there still a venture-scale business? If not, this is private equity dressed as venture.

*Source: a16z portfolio reveal; Andreessen on Cowen 2018*

---

**01.34 · Don't Pre-Optimize Monopoly** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Worry about PMF first; worry about moats second. Many great startups had no obvious moat at Series A — the moat was earned through scale, brand, and compounding data after PMF.

**Test:** If the founder cannot articulate the moat in one sentence at seed but can articulate the burning user need in one sentence — fund anyway. Reverse case is the kill.

*Source: Pmarchive: The Only Thing That Matters; vs Thiel monopoly framing*

---

**01.35 · Horizontal Platforms Are Not Dead** `a16z`

**Rule:** Thiel's 'find a small monopoly' is one valid path; Andreessen also funds horizontal platforms (cloud, dev tools, AI infra) where the prize is being a tax on a category. Both shapes can return a fund.

**Test:** Is the horizontal platform's first 100 users a coherent niche where it is the only sane choice? If yes, the horizontal is wedge-shaped. If usage is diffused across unrelated buyers, kill.

*Source: a16z portfolio: GitHub, Databricks, Stripe, Slack*

---

**01.36 · Sectors Compound; Pick One** `a16z`

**Rule:** The fund is organized by sector practice (consumer, enterprise, bio, crypto, American Dynamism, infra) because expertise compounds within a sector. Founders should match to the sector, not float across them.

**Test:** Is the founder positioned inside one sector with deep network and pattern-recognition? Cross-sector dilettante is a yellow flag.

*Source: a16z partner structure, 2015-2025*

---

**01.37 · Big-Company-Itis Is the Real Disease** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Most large incumbents fail not from lack of resources but from organizational sclerosis — process, politics, risk aversion, dilution of founder vision. Bet on insurgents against companies showing late-stage symptoms.

**Test:** Has the incumbent shipped a defining new product in the last 3 years? If the answer is 'they bought one,' the incumbent is fundable to disrupt.

*Source: Pmarchive: Big-Company-Itis; Microsoft Is Dead (PG, cited by Andreessen)*

---

**01.38 · Watch For Permissionless Innovation** `a16z`

**Rule:** The best wedges open when a regulator, gatekeeper, or distribution channel temporarily becomes permissionless — as the internet was 1995-2005, mobile 2007-2014, AI 2022-onward.

**Test:** Is there a 24-36 month window where building this is currently legal/feasible without asking permission, but will likely be regulated later? If yes, the window itself is the alpha.

*Source: Andreessen on Rogan 2023; a16z thesis posts*

---

**01.39 · Don't Confuse Cycles with Trends** `a16z`

**Rule:** Most bear-market 'tech is over' narratives are cyclical liquidity stories, not secular tech reversals. The seculars (compute, bandwidth, software penetration, AI capability) only go one way.

**Test:** Strip the macro-cycle commentary. Has the underlying tech curve flattened? If not, the bearish narrative is noise — fund through it.

*Source: Andreessen long-form interviews 2022-2024; a16z fund history*

---

**01.40 · 10x Better on the Load-Bearing Dimension** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** The product must be at least 10x better than the incumbent on the dimension users actually care about — not on a dimension you wish they cared about.

**Test:** Pick the metric the user has on a dashboard. Are you 10x better there? If you're 10x better on a metric they don't track, you don't have a product.

*Source: Pmarchive guide; a16z product reviews*

---

**01.41 · Choose Markets That Fund Their Own R&D** `Operating`

**Rule:** The strongest early-stage businesses are funded by customers, not just by VCs. Pick markets where customers will pay for capability before the product is fully baked.

**Test:** Can you sign a paid pilot with a real logo before raising the next round? If no customer will pay even at a discount, the wave isn't real yet.

*Source: Loudcloud-Opsware operating record; Anduril/Palantir-style wedge*

---

**01.42 · The Founder's Personal Pain Test** `a16z`

**Rule:** The most durable problems are ones the founder personally suffered. Pain selects for obsession; obsession selects for survival through the trough.

**Test:** Ask the founder for the day they decided to quit their job. If the story is 'I noticed a market opportunity,' lower conviction. If it's 'I couldn't stop thinking about this problem,' raise it.

*Source: a16z founder profiles; Pmarchive on hiring*

---

**01.43 · Categories Are Made, Not Found** `a16z`

**Rule:** The best companies create new categories rather than fight inside existing ones. Category creation is a marketing and product co-design problem, and demands a willingness to name the thing.

**Test:** Has the founder named the category they are creating, and can they defend it in one sentence to a journalist? If they describe the company in someone else's category, they're playing for second place.

*Source: a16z marketing playbook; Pmarchive: positioning*

---

**01.44 · Refuse Indefinite Optimism on Macro** `a16z`

**Rule:** Companies whose thesis requires a specific macro state (low rates forever, this regulatory regime continuing, this geopolitical alignment) are not investable as venture. Pick problems whose value compounds across regimes.

**Test:** Sketch the company under three macro regimes. If the company only works in one, it's a macro bet, not a startup.

*Source: Andreessen on Cowen 2022 (post-rate-shock interview)*

---

**01.45 · First-Principles Beats Best-Practices** `Essay`

**Rule:** Best-practices are crystallized cope from companies that already won. Pick problems where reasoning from first principles changes the answer — that's where the secret is.

**Test:** Ask: if no one had ever built anything in this space before, what would the obvious approach be? If that answer is different from the consensus playbook, there is alpha.

*Source: Andreessen on Lex Fridman 2024; Pmarchive guide*

---

**01.46 · The Short-the-Stagnationists Trade** `Essay`

**Rule:** Whenever consensus opinion locks onto 'this can't be done because of X coordination problem,' the highest-EV move is to build the thing that routes around X.

**Test:** Identify the loudest 'this is impossible' argument. Is the impossibility technical (real) or coordination (fake)? Bet against coordination-impossibility.

*Source: It's Time to Build; Techno-Optimist Manifesto*

---

**01.47 · Q-Test: Is This a Wave or a Ripple?** `a16z`

**Rule:** Distinguish secular waves (mobile, cloud, AI) from short cycles (web3 hype peaks, AR/VR pulses). Waves are 10+ year, multi-trillion-dollar arcs; ripples are 18-month founder-Twitter narratives.

**Test:** Plot adoption over 5 years. Is the curve still concave-up at year 3, or has it stalled? Stalled = ripple.

*Source: a16z internal pattern, 2009-2025*

## Layer 02 · Approach Taste
*Given the problem, which angle to attack from*

Once the wave is right, the playbook is operational: technical founder shipping fast, instrument PMF, recruit conspirators, capitalize ahead of the curve, and hire/fire ruthlessly. Andreessen's mechanism is iteration speed plus distribution intensity — not Thiel's secrecy and patience. The conviction comes from data, not from a master plan.

---

**02.01 · Ship the Crap Version First** `Operating`

**Rule:** Get something into users' hands within months, not years. The market teaches you what to build; the team is the iteration substrate. The cost of a year of stealth is information, not surprise.

**Test:** How long until the first paid user touches the product? If the answer is >6 months, the team is wrong about something.

*Source: Pmarchive guide; Netscape ship cadence*

---

**02.02 · Iterate Until the Market Pulls** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Pre-PMF, every meeting is about a hypothesis to invalidate. Don't decorate the product; change one big variable per cycle and measure the user-pull delta.

**Test:** Each week, what hypothesis did you invalidate? If the answer is 'we shipped polish,' the team is procrastinating on the real iteration.

*Source: Pmarchive: PMF; a16z portfolio reviews*

---

**02.03 · Instrument the User from Day One** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** You cannot improve what you cannot see. Telemetry, cohort retention, NPS, and customer interviews are part of v0, not v3.

**Test:** On day one of GA, can you draw the retention curve and the activation funnel? If not, you are flying instruments-down.

*Source: Pmarchive guide; a16z growth practice*

---

**02.04 · Hire Slow, Then Fire Fast** `Operating`

**Rule:** Take real time on the first 20 hires; once a hire is wrong, separate within weeks. The cost of carrying a B-player exceeds the cost of recruiting their replacement.

**Test:** Has anyone been a known B-player for >90 days without the founder addressing it? That's the real diagnostic for the team's seriousness.

*Source: Pmarchive: Hiring/Firing executives; Hard Thing About Hard Things*

---

**02.05 · Pay In Equity, But Pay Cash Competitively** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Below-market cash starves the team and selects for desperation, not conviction. Pay competitively in cash AND give meaningful equity — otherwise the talent flywheel dies at year two.

**Test:** Are you within 80-90% of cash market on senior roles? If you're paying 50% and 'making it up in equity,' you are losing the people you most need.

*Source: Pmarchive: comp series*

---

**02.06 · The 60-Second Board Meeting Rule** `Operating`

**Rule:** Don't waste board meetings reading slides. Send the deck 48 hours ahead, expect everyone to have read it, and use the live time only for decisions and disagreements.

**Test:** Does the first 60 seconds of your board meeting include any slide-reading? If yes, you are losing one of the highest-leverage hours in the company.

*Source: a16z board practices; Hard Thing About Hard Things*

---

**02.07 · Disagree and Commit** `Operating`

**Rule:** After a real argument, the team aligns and executes — even those who lost the argument. Lingering dissent at execution time is corrosive; if the disagreement is too deep to commit, the person is in the wrong seat.

**Test:** Two weeks after a contested decision, is the team executing as if it were unanimous? If not, the founder failed the post-decision integration step.

*Source: Marc & Ben management practices*

---

**02.08 · Sell Hard, Price High** `Operating`

**Rule:** Founders systematically underprice and underinvest in sales. The first heuristic on a B2B price is 'double it and see who flinches' — buyers signal quality through cost.

**Test:** Have you tested a 2x price with five qualified prospects? If you haven't even tried, you don't know your willingness-to-pay curve.

*Source: Pmarchive: pricing; Loudcloud-Opsware enterprise sales*

---

**02.09 · Pay Salespeople Like Founders** `Operating`

**Rule:** Top enterprise reps are founder-tier rare. Compensate accordingly — uncapped commission, generous equity, real autonomy — or you'll lose them mid-cycle.

**Test:** Is your top rep paid more than your VP Eng? In high-ACV enterprise, that's normal — if not, your comp model assumes sales is a commodity it isn't.

*Source: Pmarchive: sales; Opsware enterprise playbook*

---

**02.10 · Distribution Is the Differentiator** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** At equal product quality, the company with better distribution wins every time. Treat distribution as a product, not as a marketing line item.

**Test:** Name the single dominant channel and the metric proving it. If the answer is 'we do a bit of everything,' the distribution motion is uninvented.

*Source: Pmarchive: distribution; a16z portfolio reviews*

---

**02.11 · Concentrate Marketing Spend** `a16z`

**Rule:** Spread marketing across many channels = noise across many channels. Pick one channel where you can dominate share-of-voice in your category, and over-spend there.

**Test:** Does a target customer encounter your brand 4-7 times in a 30-day window inside the dominant channel? If 1-2, you are spreading too thin.

*Source: a16z marketing playbook*

---

**02.12 · Tell the Story Loudly** `a16z`

**Rule:** Marc-and-Ben thesis: Silicon Valley undertells its own story. Founders who refuse to publicly explain themselves cede the narrative to skeptics. Talk on podcasts, post threads, name the category.

**Test:** Could a journalist write a one-paragraph summary of what your company is and why it matters from your public output? If no, you have a comms gap that becomes a hiring gap that becomes a regulator gap.

*Source: a16z podcast 2018-2025; Andreessen on X*

---

**02.13 · Capitalize Ahead of the Curve** `a16z`

**Rule:** Raise more than you think you need, especially in winters. Capital is a weapon — undercapitalized companies die at the inflection point, even when their thesis is right.

**Test:** Do you have 24 months of runway through the next plausible capital winter? If under 18 months at peak burn, raise now, not later.

*Source: Andreessen on Cowen 2022; a16z dry-powder posture*

---

**02.14 · Default Big** `a16z`

**Rule:** Aim at category-defining outcomes from day one — small ambitions select for small markets, small teams, and small outcomes. The behavioral cost of thinking big is near-zero; the upside cost of thinking small is everything.

**Test:** If the company succeeds beyond expectations, what is it in 10 years? If the answer is a $100M business, it isn't venture-fundable.

*Source: a16z thesis; Andreessen on Lex Fridman 2024*

---

**02.15 · Founder/CEO Is the Job, Not a Phase** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** The technical founder should remain CEO through scale, with deliberate executive coaching — not be replaced by 'adult supervision.' The board's job is to develop the founder, not to remove them.

**Test:** Is the board's most recent conversation about CEO development positive (training, coaching) or negative (succession)? Negative is a leading indicator of company decline.

*Source: Pmarchive: hiring/managing executives; Founder Mode (PG, endorsed by Andreessen) 2024*

---

**02.16 · Skip-Level Operator Mode** `Operating`

**Rule:** The CEO must engage 1-2 levels below their direct reports — not to micromanage, but to maintain ground truth. Founders who manage only their managers are the first to be lied to.

**Test:** When you skip-level, do you learn anything you didn't already know? If never, your direct reports are filtering aggressively. If sometimes, your operating cadence is healthy.

*Source: Founder Mode endorsement, 2024; a16z podcast*

---

**02.17 · Coach the Founder, Don't Replace Them** `a16z`

**Rule:** The right move when a technical founder hits an operating ceiling is intensive coaching, not replacement. Replacement signals to the next generation of founders that ambition is unwelcome.

**Test:** Has the board invested in real coaching (CEO whisperer, executive coach, peer founder network) before any conversation about replacement? If not, the board is lazy.

*Source: Marc & Ben CEO coaching practice 2009-2025*

---

**02.18 · Two Pizzas, Three Tables, One Mission** `Operating`

**Rule:** The most productive teams are tight units of 6-12 people who can repeat the thesis unprompted and disagree productively. Beyond ~12, communication overhead overwhelms execution speed.

**Test:** If you split the team into pods, does each pod have a thesis-carrier who can re-derive the company's strategy from first principles? If not, you have employees, not operators.

*Source: Pmarchive: org design; Loudcloud-era teams*

---

**02.19 · Recruit From Personal Network First** `a16z`

**Rule:** The first 20 hires should come from the founders' personal networks. Recruiters can scale a hiring function but cannot manufacture trust.

**Test:** What % of the first 20 hires came from a founder's direct relationship? Below ~70%, the founders' network is too thin or they're outsourcing the most important decision.

*Source: a16z portfolio analyses*

---

**02.20 · Use The Network as a Service** `a16z`

**Rule:** An a16z-style fund's deepest value is not capital — it is the introduction graph (customers, recruits, regulators, journalists). Treat the firm's network as the operating extension of the company.

**Test:** Are you using your investors' network monthly? If you only show up at board meetings, you're underusing the most expensive resource on the cap table.

*Source: a16z platform model, 2009-2025*

---

**02.21 · Sell to a Forced Buyer Early** `a16z`

**Rule:** The early customer who pays for capability — not price — funds the R&D loop. In American Dynamism, this is the DoD or DoE; in enterprise software, it's the company with an existential pain.

**Test:** Can you name the buyer with the burning fire whose career is on the line? If 'lots of buyers might want this,' you don't yet have a wedge.

*Source: Anduril, Palantir, SpaceX comparable patterns; a16z thesis*

---

**02.22 · Land-and-Expand Pricing** `Operating`

**Rule:** In enterprise, get inside the account at any price — then expand seats, modules, and contract scope from a position of incumbency. Initial price is a marketing variable; lifetime value is the real number.

**Test:** What is the projected ratio of year-3 ACV to year-1 ACV per logo? If <2x, the product isn't sticky enough or the rep isn't expansion-incentivized.

*Source: Opsware enterprise playbook; a16z enterprise reviews*

---

**02.23 · Build the Pivot Capability, Not the Pivot** `Operating`

**Rule:** Loudcloud-to-Opsware is the textbook pivot — same founders, same insight, totally different artifact. Build a team that can survive a thesis-shift, not one that's wedded to today's product.

**Test:** If your core market disappeared in 6 months, would the team intact pivot — or dissolve? The answer tests whether the team is bonded to people or to a product.

*Source: Loudcloud→Opsware operating record, 1999-2007*

---

**02.24 · Be Formidable in Fundraising** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Investors fund traction or formidability. Pre-traction, the founder must be visibly impossible to dismiss — deep on the problem, articulate on the wave, intolerant of incrementalism.

**Test:** Does the founder set the agenda of the pitch meeting, or react to the investor's questions? If the latter, formidability is missing.

*Source: Pmarchive: investors; Andreessen on Lex 2023*

---

**02.25 · Treat the Press as a Distribution Channel** `a16z`

**Rule:** Earned media is venture-grade leverage when used deliberately. Pick the journalist, pick the angle, time it to a real product moment — don't rely on PR firms to invent narrative.

**Test:** Can you name the three journalists who will cover your category and have a personal relationship with each? If no, your earned-media channel is essentially closed.

*Source: a16z marketing playbook, 2010-2025*

---

**02.26 · Engage Regulators Early** `a16z`

**Rule:** In atoms-heavy or politically-loaded sectors, regulators are part of the product. Found a regulatory function inside the company; treat the FDA/NRC/DoD/SEC as an external customer to be educated.

**Test:** Has the founder met the relevant regulator face-to-face before Series A? If you'll deal with regulators 'later,' later usually means after a setback that costs 12 months.

*Source: American Dynamism portfolio; Anduril/Boom/Oklo patterns*

---

**02.27 · Vertical Integration When the Stack Is Broken** `Operating`

**Rule:** When existing suppliers or platforms can't deliver, eat the stack. Vertical integration is expensive; it's also the only way some products can exist.

**Test:** Have you tried buying from external suppliers and been told 'we can't do that for 18 months'? If yes, build it yourself; the optionality you lose to suppliers is the company.

*Source: Loudcloud lessons; SpaceX/Anduril comparables*

---

**02.28 · Pay Down Tech Debt After PMF, Not Before** `Operating`

**Rule:** Pre-PMF, the codebase is disposable scaffolding. Post-PMF, you must invest in engineering excellence aggressively — the v1 'good enough' becomes the bottleneck on growth within ~12 months.

**Test:** Are you in pre-PMF or post-PMF, and is your engineering investment matched? Pre: ship cycles dominated by feature work. Post: ~30%+ of eng time on platform, infra, and refactoring.

*Source: Pmarchive: engineering; a16z scaling playbook*

---

**02.29 · Refuse the Outsourced CTO** `a16z`

**Rule:** Outsourcing core engineering at the seed stage ensures the team cannot iterate fast enough to find PMF. The team that builds v1 is the team that survives the next two years.

**Test:** Is the v1 product shipped by employees or by a contracting agency? If the latter, the iteration loop will not survive contact with users.

*Source: Pmarchive: hiring; a16z portfolio diligence*

---

**02.30 · Defensive Hiring Is Suicidal** `a16z`

**Rule:** Hiring people 'safer than the founders' to make investors comfortable is the surest path to mediocrity. Founders should hire weirder, not safer.

**Test:** Of the last five hires, how many would have been rejected by the average BigCo recruiting funnel? If zero, you are recruiting BigCo employees and will become BigCo.

*Source: Pmarchive: hiring; a16z founder profiles*

---

**02.31 · Force Function the Roadmap** `Operating`

**Rule:** Public commitments — to customers, to press, to the team — accelerate decision-making. Internal-only roadmaps drift; externally-committed roadmaps ship.

**Test:** Has the company publicly committed to a delivery date in the last 90 days? Healthy companies do this often; calcifying ones never do.

*Source: Pmarchive: discipline; Netscape ship cadence*

---

**02.32 · Walk the Customer Through Buying** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** The founder should personally walk a real customer through the buying flow — every hand-off, every signature, every onboarding step — quarterly. Friction the founder doesn't feel becomes friction the customer doesn't survive.

**Test:** When did the founder last sit through a complete buying flow? If >90 days, ground truth has decayed.

*Source: Pmarchive: distribution; a16z product reviews*

---

**02.33 · Pricing Lever Is the Cheapest Lever** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Pricing changes are higher-leverage than feature work and cheaper than ad spend, yet most founders touch them once a year. Re-test pricing every quarter until you have a real PMF signal.

**Test:** How many price experiments have you run in the last 12 months? Below 4, you are leaving the easiest gains on the table.

*Source: Pmarchive: pricing series*

---

**02.34 · Ship a Wedge, Then a Suite** `a16z`

**Rule:** Win in a small slice with a 10x wedge product, then expand into adjacent modules from a position of trust. Suite-from-day-one is the dominant failure mode of ambitious founders.

**Test:** Can a customer say in one sentence what your wedge product does? If they list five things, you have a suite, not a wedge.

*Source: a16z enterprise playbook*

---

**02.35 · Run Toward Hard Problems** `Essay`

**Rule:** The competitive advantage is in the work that nobody else wants to do — regulator engagement, hardware QA, enterprise procurement, on-call escalations. Soft problems are commoditized.

**Test:** What's the most unglamorous thing your company does well? If you can't name one, your moat is shallow.

*Source: It's Time to Build; American Dynamism essays*

---

**02.36 · Strong Opinions, Loosely Held** `Essay`

**Rule:** Have a strong thesis; revise on evidence. Founders without opinions can't decide; founders who refuse to update can't survive the second product cycle.

**Test:** When did the founder last publicly change a major thesis on the basis of new data? If never, they are either always right or always rationalizing.

*Source: Andreessen on Cowen 2018; common Andreessen line*

---

**02.37 · Optionality Is For Cowards** `Essay`

**Rule:** Hedging across multiple products, geographies, or theses pre-PMF is procrastination dressed as risk management. Concentrate; the only real hedge is execution speed.

**Test:** Is the company running 2+ unrelated bets at seed? Kill all but one. If the founder refuses, conviction is missing.

*Source: Andreessen on Lex Fridman 2024*

---

**02.38 · Conquerors, Not Victims** `Essay`

**Rule:** Victim mentality is a curse in every domain — including the relationship to technology, regulators, and competition. The founder's posture is conqueror: undertake the Hero's Journey, rebel against the status quo, bring home the spoils. Complaint is not strategy.

**Test:** When the founder describes a setback, do they assign agency to themselves, or to the regulator/market/incumbent? Externalized blame is a leading indicator of stalled execution.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Becoming Technological Supermen section)*

---

**02.39 · Reject Resentment** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per the manifesto's Carrie Fisher line — 'Resentment is like drinking poison and waiting for the other person to die.' Founders and teams operating from ressentiment toward incumbents, the press, or 'the system' burn cycles on grievance instead of building. Take responsibility and overcome.

**Test:** How much of the founder's narrative is about what enemies are doing to them vs what they are building? Above ~20% grievance share, the team's energy is leaking.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technological Values; The Enemy)*

---

**02.40 · Challenge Credentialed Experts** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per Feynman (cited in the manifesto): 'Science is the belief in the ignorance of experts.' The right disposition is free inquiry, scientific method, and willingness to challenge the credentialed consensus when the data warrants. Founders who genuflect to expert opinion before testing it lose the alpha.

**Test:** When the consensus expert position contradicts the founder's local data, does the founder defer or test? If they defer, they will be out-iterated by founders who test.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technological Values, citing Feynman)*

---

**02.41 · Local Knowledge at the Edges** `Essay`

**Rule:** All actual information lives at the edges, with the people closest to the buyer. The center, abstracted from buyer and seller, knows nothing. Build operating cadences (dogfooding, user calls, frontline shadowing) that route ground truth past middle management.

**Test:** Does the founder talk to actual users every week, or do they read summaries from a CS team? Summaries are filtered; calls are signal.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Markets section, Hayek's Knowledge Problem)*

---

**02.42 · Pay It Forward** `Essay`

**Rule:** The Silicon Valley code is generosity of spirit, trust via aligned incentives, helping each other learn and grow. Founders and firms that hoard introductions, knowledge, and capital underperform on the long arc — the network compounds for those who give first.

**Test:** How many introductions, candid memos, or warm referrals did the founder make in the last 90 days that did not directly serve their company? Zero is a yellow flag.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technological Values section)*

---

**02.43 · Ambition, Aggression, Persistence** `Essay`

**Rule:** The manifesto's stated values stack — ambition, aggression, persistence, relentlessness, strength — are operating-tempo settings, not personality traits. Sustained tempo is the moat. Calm professionalism without intensity loses to scrappy intensity every time.

**Test:** What is the team's weekly ship cadence? If big-co rhythms (quarterly, slow code review, no on-call) dominate at <50 people, the tempo is wrong for the wave.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Technological Values section)*

## Layer 03 · Stopping Taste
*When to abandon, when to push, when done enough*

Andreessen's stopping rules are growth-shaped and cohort-shaped — not narrative-shaped. Hold winners aggressively, follow on into the power-law outcomes, kill the indeterminate ones early, and never let a board fight ride past the point of marginal influence. Where Thiel exits rarely and concentrates, Andreessen plays the portfolio at scale.

---

**03.01 · Power Law of Outcomes** `a16z`

**Rule:** Venture returns are dominated by 1-2 outliers per fund. Invest and follow on accordingly: the rule is not 'don't lose,' it's 'don't miss the outlier when it's in your hand.'

**Test:** Of the bottom-quartile portfolio companies, would killing them and redeploying meaningfully change fund-level returns? If no (it usually doesn't), don't waste energy trimming them — focus on the top quartile.

*Source: a16z fund history; Andreessen on Cowen 2018*

---

**03.02 · Follow On Into Winners** `a16z`

**Rule:** When a portfolio company is breaking out, the right move is to write the largest check the fund's structure allows. Reserves are for winners, not for triage.

**Test:** Look at the last 10 follow-on checks. Were they concentrated in the top 20% by traction? If reserves were spread evenly, the fund is losing the power-law game.

*Source: a16z reserve practice 2009-2025*

---

**03.03 · Kill Indeterminate Bets Early** `a16z`

**Rule:** An investment that's neither breaking out nor failing — but consuming attention — is the most expensive thing in the portfolio. Cut early; release the capital and the cognitive load.

**Test:** Has this company shown convex growth or a clean kill signal in the last 12 months? If neither, the right move is to discuss exit, not to wait.

*Source: a16z portfolio reviews; Hard Thing About Hard Things*

---

**03.04 · Don't Trim Outliers** `a16z`

**Rule:** Selling early in winners — for partial liquidity or 'taking some off the table' — is the single largest source of regret in venture. Hold to true escape velocity.

**Test:** If you sell at 5x, what is the probability you regret it in 5 years? In venture, the answer is usually >50%. Default to holding.

*Source: a16z secondary policy; Andreessen on Cowen 2018*

---

**03.05 · PMF or Pivot — No Third Option** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Pre-PMF companies are in one of two states: chasing PMF, or pivoting toward it. 'Continuing the plan' without traction is denial — pivot or wind down within ~24 months.

**Test:** How many months has the company been pre-PMF? If >24 with the same plan, the team is either pivoting silently or in denial. Force the conversation.

*Source: Pmarchive: PMF; a16z portfolio diligence*

---

**03.06 · The 5-7% Weekly Pull Test** `a16z`

**Rule:** In consumer/SMB, sustained 5-7% week-over-week organic growth is the cleanest PMF signal. Below that, the wave hasn't arrived; declare it openly and pivot or kill.

**Test:** What is the trailing 12-week organic growth rate? Sub-5% w/w sustained = pre-PMF; founder must pivot or accept the kill.

*Source: Common a16z growth bar (echoing PG's 'Startup = Growth')*

---

**03.07 · Net Dollar Retention Floor** `a16z`

**Rule:** In enterprise SaaS, NDR <100% means the product is leaking faster than it lands — no marketing budget can save it. Above 120% the company is a compounder; in between is a danger zone that demands product investment.

**Test:** What is trailing-12-month NDR? Below 100, halt growth spend and fix the leak. Above 120, double sales spend.

*Source: a16z enterprise diagnostics, 2015-2025*

---

**03.08 · Cash Out at Generational Highs** `a16z`

**Rule:** When the public market is paying generational multiples for your category and the company can compound just as well private, take real liquidity for the founders and early team. The risk of zero is non-trivial; the cost of waiting can be everything.

**Test:** Is the public comps multiple at >90th percentile of its 10-year history? If yes, secondaries for founders are prudent — not a sell signal for the company.

*Source: a16z secondaries practice; cycle-aware comp analysis*

---

**03.09 · Stay Private While Capital Is Available** `a16z`

**Rule:** Don't IPO under pressure when private capital is abundant; the public market is a one-way door that imposes quarterly behavior on long-cycle businesses.

**Test:** Can the company raise 24+ months of growth capital privately at a fair price? If yes, IPO is optional; the right reason to IPO is liquidity for employees, not capital.

*Source: a16z late-stage strategy 2015-2025*

---

**03.10 · Replace Reluctantly, Coach First** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** When an executive is failing, coach intensely for one cycle. If still failing, replace within weeks — not months. The mid-state (ambiguous, slow-walked) corrodes the team faster than either coach or fire.

**Test:** How long has there been performance ambiguity at the exec level? If >90 days, the founder is procrastinating on the decision and paying the org-tax for it.

*Source: Pmarchive: hiring/firing executives*

---

**03.11 · Refuse the Perpetual-Pivot Trap** `a16z`

**Rule:** Three+ pivots in a year usually means the team has lost conviction on what the company is for. Either the founders re-anchor on a thesis they actually believe — or wind down.

**Test:** Count pivots in the last 18 months. >2 substantive pivots = the team is exploring uncertainty, not executing on conviction. Force a decision.

*Source: a16z portfolio diagnostics; Ning era lessons*

---

**03.12 · Ning Lesson: Platforms Need Wedges** `Operating`

**Rule:** Horizontal platforms without a beachhead vertical die slowly. If the platform serves 'everyone,' it will lose to a vertical-specific tool in every category.

**Test:** Name the top-3 use cases by usage. If they're unrelated and none is dominant, the platform is unanchored — pick a vertical and win it before re-broadening.

*Source: Ning operating record, 2005-2011*

---

**03.13 · Don't Fight Locked Boards** `Operating`

**Rule:** When governance is structurally against you (super-voting blocks, hostile chair, dominant institutional voting), marginal influence is below marginal time-cost. Disengage gracefully.

**Test:** Run the vote. If you lose 4-1 on three consecutive material decisions, your seat is decorative. Step down and convert to advisor or sell.

*Source: a16z board patterns; Hard Thing About Hard Things*

---

**03.14 · Founders Quit Slow, Then All At Once** `Operating`

**Rule:** Founders who are quietly burning out are a 6-12 month leading indicator of a crisis. Investor's job is to spot it early — energy/health/relationship signals — and intervene, not to wait for the resignation email.

**Test:** How does the founder describe their own energy in monthly check-ins? If 'fine, fine' becomes monosyllabic, get on a plane.

*Source: Marc & Ben CEO support practice*

---

**03.15 · Refund and Restart > Slow Death** `a16z`

**Rule:** If the thesis is dead but the team is great and capital remains, return the money and let the team spin out a new bet — rather than zombie-running a known-dead plan.

**Test:** Has the team had a real conviction conversation about whether the thesis is alive? If not, schedule it. Slow-death runs are bad for everyone.

*Source: a16z wind-down practice; common in YC corpus*

---

**03.16 · Liquidity Begets Liquidity** `a16z`

**Rule:** Founders who take partial founder secondaries early — when the company is breaking out — are statistically more likely to keep building. Wealth-anxiety distorts long-cycle decisions.

**Test:** Has the founder taken a meaningful secondary by Series C in a breakout? If not, watch for risk aversion creeping into product decisions; the founder may be effectively all-in personally.

*Source: a16z secondaries practice 2012-2025*

---

**03.17 · Sell to a Strategic Only at Generational Multiples** `a16z`

**Rule:** Most strategic acquisition offers undervalue the long arc. Default to declining; accept only when the multiple is generational AND the team has lost taste for the next 10-year arc.

**Test:** Compare the offer to your 10-year DCF on the optimistic case. If the offer is below 50% of that, decline reflexively.

*Source: Andreessen on Rogan 2023; FB-Yahoo precedent (cited)*

---

**03.18 · Cyclical Bear ≠ Secular Reversal** `a16z`

**Rule:** In a tech bear market, founders panic-pivot and investors mark down indiscriminately. The discipline is to look at the secular curve (compute, software penetration, AI capability) and act on it — not on the cycle.

**Test:** Has the underlying tech curve flattened, or just the market multiple? If only multiple, hold and follow on; if curve, reconsider.

*Source: Andreessen on Cowen 2022; a16z 2022-2024 posture*

---

**03.19 · Stop Selling When Customers Re-Order** `a16z`

**Rule:** Once cohorts re-order without sales involvement, redirect sales spend from acquisition to expansion. Continuing acquisition spend post-PMF wastes the channel that's now free.

**Test:** What % of new revenue is coming from existing customer expansion? Above ~40%, you're in the expansion phase — re-cut the sales comp plan.

*Source: a16z enterprise scaling playbook*

---

**03.20 · Wind Down Wars of Attrition** `Operating`

**Rule:** Two-equal-quality competitors in the same niche eat each other's margins for years. Either consolidate (M&A), differentiate sharply, or exit — don't half-fight.

**Test:** Have the two of you been pricing within 10% for 18 months? You're in a war of attrition. Pick one of: merge, differentiate, exit.

*Source: Hard Thing About Hard Things; common a16z playbook*

---

**03.21 · Refuse Vanity Fundraises** `a16z`

**Rule:** Raising at a too-high valuation for press value invites an inevitable down round, which kills morale and recruiting. Price the round at a number you can grow into within 18 months.

**Test:** Is the post-money 2x your defensible 18-month forward multiple? If yes, you're courting a future down round.

*Source: Pmarchive: investors; a16z late-stage practice*

---

**03.22 · Thesis Debate Stays in the Room** `Operating`

**Rule:** Disagreements about strategy are healthy; airing them publicly destroys recruiting and customer trust. Argue inside, present unanimously outside.

**Test:** Has any internal strategy disagreement leaked publicly in the last quarter? If yes, the team's information hygiene is broken — fix the leak before fixing the strategy.

*Source: Marc & Ben management practices*

---

**03.23 · Don't Optimize Indefinite Optionality** `a16z`

**Rule:** 'Keeping options open' is a tell for founders who don't yet know what to commit to. After 12 months in market, the founder must commit to one thesis and abandon the others — even if all three look fundable.

**Test:** Is the company explicitly betting on one thesis with all of its top-five hires? If hires are split across theses, the founder hasn't yet decided.

*Source: Andreessen on Cowen 2022; vs Thiel 'definite optimist' overlap*

---

**03.24 · Recognize the Big-Company-Itis Inflection** `Pmarchive`

**Rule:** Once the company has VPs of VPs, slide-decks-to-discuss-slide-decks, and a head of brand, the founder's job becomes preventing organizational sclerosis — not closing the next deal.

**Test:** How many internal meetings exist primarily to prepare for other internal meetings? Above ~3 standing meetings of that shape, you have entered late-stage symptoms.

*Source: Pmarchive: Big-Company-Itis; Founder Mode 2024*

---

**03.25 · Sell Yahoo, Buy AOL — But Know Which Is Which** `Operating`

**Rule:** Some acquisitions buy time (AOL bought Netscape strategically); some destroy value (Yahoo's serial misacquisitions). Distinguish acquisitions that protect the long-arc thesis from those that bury it.

**Test:** After the acquisition, is the founder still in operating control of the thesis? If no, the acquisition is most likely a slow shutdown wearing a press release.

*Source: Netscape-AOL operating record; cross-reference Yahoo decline*

---

**03.26 · Don't Confuse 'Mature' with 'Done'** `Essay`

**Rule:** A growing public company is not a finished company. The question 'are we done growing' is a leading indicator of decline; the right question is always 'what's the next compounding curve?'

**Test:** Has the leadership named a credible 5-10x growth path from today's revenue? If not, the company is grooming itself to be acquired, not built.

*Source: Microsoft Is Dead (PG, cited); Andreessen on Rogan*

---

**03.27 · The Founder's Energy Is the Cap** `Operating`

**Rule:** Long-arc companies survive on founder stamina more than founder strategy. Protect sleep, marriages, health — without these, no doctrine in this playbook fires.

**Test:** What is the founder's average sleep over the last 30 days? Below ~6h sustained, the company is rate-limited by physiology, not strategy.

*Source: Andreessen on Tim Ferriss; Marc & Ben CEO coaching practice*

---

**03.28 · Cut the Last-Man Founder** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per Nietzsche (quoted at length in the manifesto): the Last Man wants comfort, has discovered 'happiness,' no longer despises himself, and will not give birth to any star. A founder who has slid into Last-Man comfort — risk-averse, careful, optimizing for not being hurt — has stopped being a founder. Replace, coach hard, or wind down.

**Test:** When did the founder last take a serious risk that could have failed publicly? If >12 months of strictly safe choices, the founder is in Last-Man drift; intervene.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (The Enemy section, citing Nietzsche)*

---

**03.29 · Refuse the Precautionary Principle** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per the manifesto: the Precautionary Principle 'would have prevented virtually all progress since man first harnessed fire' and is 'deeply immoral.' When a stop-decision is made on precautionary grounds — 'we don't know enough yet, let's wait' — the burden of proof is on stopping, not on building. Default to ship.

**Test:** Is the company stalling on a precautionary argument (unknown long-term effects, theoretical edge case, social-license worry)? If the worst-case is bounded and reversible, ship; if unbounded and physical, evaluate — but never defer because of a generic precautionary frame.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (The Enemy section)*

---

**03.30 · Slouching Toward Utopia, Not Toward It** `Essay`

**Rule:** Per Brad DeLong (cited in the manifesto): the goal is doing the best fallen humanity can do, making things better as we go — not reaching a final end-state. Stopping criteria pegged to 'we have not yet achieved the perfect outcome' are a category error; ship the marginal improvement and iterate.

**Test:** Is a release being held because it does not yet hit a utopian internal bar? If the marginal version is clearly better than the status quo, ship — let the market grade the marginal step.

*Source: Techno-Optimist Manifesto, October 2023 (Not Utopia But Close Enough section, citing DeLong)*
