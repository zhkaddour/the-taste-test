## Layer 1 — Problem-Selection Taste *(13 doctrines)*
*Where to invest — finding transformative innovation.*

Cathie Wood's investment doctrine is built on a single insight: genuinely transformative technologies follow predictable S-curve adoption patterns with dramatically falling costs. The investor who recognizes these technologies before the S-curve inflects — when the technology is still in the disbelief or skepticism phase — earns returns that are structurally unavailable to the consensus investor. The filter for where to invest is not 'what is currently popular' but 'what technology will seem obvious in 10 years that looks premature today?'

### From the ARK Big Ideas corpus and Lex Fridman interview (2021)

- **The Five Innovation Platforms** *(ARK Big Ideas 2017-2025; ARK investment philosophy white papers)* — The transformative technologies of the current era are organized around five platforms: (1) DNA sequencing and genomics, (2) robotics and autonomous systems, (3) energy storage and EVs, (4) artificial intelligence, (5) blockchain/cryptocurrency. All other technology innovation is either a subset of these or does not meet the transformative threshold. Investment should concentrate on these five and their intersections.
- **Disruptive Innovation Definition** *(ARK Big Ideas 2017; ARK investment philosophy; Lex Fridman interview 2021)* — Disruptive innovation meets four criteria: (1) enabled by technology, not just business model innovation; (2) crosses major adoption curves from early adopter to mainstream; (3) dramatic cost declines over time via Wright's Law dynamics; (4) solves previously unsolvable problems. All four must be present.
- **Wright's Law — The Cost Curve Is the Thesis** *(ARK Big Ideas 2017-2024; ARK Tesla model methodology)* — Wright's Law: for every cumulative doubling of production, unit costs fall by a consistent percentage. EVs, solar panels, DNA sequencing, and battery storage all follow Wright's Law trajectories. The investment thesis is not primarily about current economics — it is about where the cost curve will be in 5 years, given observed production volumes and historical cost-reduction rates.
- **Consensus Is Priced In** *(ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood interviews 2020-2024)* — The investor whose thesis matches the consensus earns consensus returns — which are average by definition. Alpha requires a thesis that diverges from consensus in a direction that turns out to be correct. If the buy-side consensus agrees with your thesis, the opportunity has already closed.
- **The 5-Year Horizon Minimum** *(ARK Big Ideas annual reports; Cathie Wood interviews; Lex Fridman 2021)* — Disruptive innovation takes 5-10 years to move from early to mainstream adoption. Quarterly earnings reports, short-term revenue growth, and near-term profitability are poor signals for transformative companies. Any shorter horizon will systematically undervalue transformative companies.
- **Platform Convergence Multiplies Returns** *(ARK Big Ideas 2022-2025; ARK convergence thesis documents)* — The largest opportunities arise where two or more of the five innovation platforms converge. AI + genomics = drug discovery at unprecedented speed. AI + robotics = physical world automation. Each convergence creates a market that no single platform could create alone.
- **Invest in the Disbelief and Skepticism Phases** *(ARK investment philosophy; Gartner Hype Cycle analogy; Cathie Wood on market psychology)* — Technology adoption follows an emotional arc: disbelief → skepticism → acceptance → enthusiasm. The largest returns accrue to investors who enter in the disbelief or skepticism phase. By acceptance, the thesis is known; by enthusiasm, it's priced.
- **Category Creation vs. Category Competition** *(ARK Big Ideas; Tesla investment thesis evolution 2014-2025)* — The transformative companies create categories that don't yet exist. Tesla didn't compete in the automobile market — it created the EV market. Category creators earn structurally better economics than category competitors.
- **The Market Doesn't Understand Yet Arbitrage** *(Cathie Wood interviews on institutional constraints; ARK investment philosophy)* — The most reliable source of alpha is the window of time between when a technology becomes genuinely promising and when the broad institutional market recognizes it. Career risk prevents most institutional investors from participating — which keeps prices low.
- **The Open Research Model as Competitive Advantage** *(ARK open research model; Cathie Wood on transparency as strategy)* — Publishing research, models, and theses publicly is not charity — it is a competitive strategy. Publishing attracts the best analysts and guest contributors, builds trust with investors, and forces rigor. Transparency is itself a moat when most competitors are opaque.
- **The Innovation Lifecycle: From Disbelief to Bubble to Real Value** *(ARK Big Ideas 2021-2023; Cathie Wood on the crypto bear market; dot-com analogy)* — Transformative technologies often go through a bubble phase before reaching the phase where real value is created. The dot-com bubble was a bubble; the internet was not a failed technology. Distinguish the bubble from the underlying technology.
- **The Institutional Constraint Arbitrage** *(ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood on institutional constraints)* — Most institutional investors face constraints ARK does not: benchmark tracking, rating-agency-driven mandates, quarterly redemption risk, career risk. These constraints systematically exclude the best early-stage disruptive investments, creating a structural pricing opportunity.
- **The 'What Does AI Unlock Here?' Question** *(ARK Big Ideas 2023-2025; Cathie Wood on AI convergence with other platforms)* — Artificial intelligence is a general-purpose technology that will materially accelerate progress in every other innovation platform. The question for any domain: what problems are currently gated by the speed of human cognition or the cost of analysis, and how does AI change that gate?

## Layer 2 — Approach Taste *(13 doctrines)*
*How to invest — conviction, analysis, and portfolio construction.*

ARK's investment approach is built around three non-standard convictions: (1) the correct benchmark is absolute return over a 5-year horizon, not relative return over a quarter; (2) conviction must be built through independent analysis, not synthesized from consensus; (3) the correct response to a thesis-affirming price decline is to buy more, not to exit. Each of these convictions contradicts mainstream institutional practice — which is why they can be a source of alpha.

### From the ARK research methodology and portfolio construction principles

- **The Three-Scenario Model** *(ARK Tesla price target models; ARK Bitcoin price target memos; ARK research methodology)* — For every major position, build a bull case, base case, and bear case price target — each with explicit assumptions about technology adoption, cost curves, and market penetration. The expected value is probability-weighted across scenarios. This framework makes the thesis falsifiable and the assumptions transparent.
- **Conviction Holding — Add on Thesis-Affirming Declines** *(ARK 2022 drawdown and response; Cathie Wood on conviction investing; Lex Fridman 2021)* — When a stock falls because the market doesn't understand the innovation thesis — not because the thesis has changed — the correct response is to increase the position size. The thesis hasn't changed; the expected return has increased.
- **Reject Benchmark-Relative Thinking** *(ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood on benchmark risk)* — Benchmark-relative investors ask 'how much did we underperform the S&P this quarter?' Absolute-return investors ask 'what will this position be worth in 5 years?' The benchmark frame will systematically cause you to exit positions that are tracking your 5-year thesis but lagging the index in the short term.
- **Interview the Inventors, Not the Executives** *(ARK research process; Cathie Wood on expert network as research edge)* — The most reliable information about a technology's potential comes from the people doing the research — not from executives, investor relations teams, or sell-side analysts. Their assessment of what's possible is more valuable than any financial model built from public filings.
- **The Cost Curve Projection Method** *(ARK Big Ideas methodology; ARK Tesla battery model; ARK solar model)* — The core analytical tool: identify the relevant technology's unit cost over the last 10+ years, fit a Wright's Law curve to the data, project forward using volume assumptions. The projected cost in Year 5 determines what markets become addressable and what margins become achievable.
- **The 'Disbelief, Skepticism, Acceptance, Enthusiasm' Timing Framework** *(ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood on market psychology)* — ARK's entry timing maps to the emotional adoption cycle. The sweet spot is early in skepticism: enough evidence exists that the technology is real, but not enough for institutional investors to participate. Enthusiasm = late; disbelief = too early; skepticism = optimal.
- **Concentration in the Highest-Conviction Positions** *(ARK portfolio construction; Cathie Wood on concentration vs. diversification)* — ARK runs concentrated portfolios by design. Diversification for its own sake dilutes the returns from the highest-conviction positions. The analyst who has identified 3 truly exceptional opportunities should concentrate there, not spread equally across 50 positions to reduce tracking error.
- **Public Models as Intellectual Honesty** *(ARK Tesla price target publications; ARK Bitcoin price targets; ARK research methodology)* — By publishing price target models publicly, ARK creates accountability for its analysis. Public models can be criticized, improved, and proven wrong. This feedback loop improves analysis quality. Proprietary models that are never tested against public scrutiny accumulate bias and error.
- **Price Targets Are 5-Year Present Values** *(ARK price target methodology; Tesla, Bitcoin, and Zoom models)* — ARK's price targets are 5-year discounted cash flow estimates, not next-quarter consensus price targets. A stock can be at $200 today and have a 5-year price target of $1,000 — the intervening path matters less than the destination.
- **The Open Ecosystem of Research Contributors** *(ARK open research platform; ARK guest contributor model)* — ARK's open research platform — where domain experts can contribute analysis publicly — creates an ecosystem of intelligence that a closed research team cannot replicate. The best people in each domain will contribute to frameworks that are open and intellectually honest.
- **The S-Curve Entry Point** *(ARK Big Ideas; Wright's Law and S-curve relationship; ARK adoption curve analysis)* — Technology adoption follows an S-curve: slow initial adoption, then an inflection to rapid growth, then maturation. The highest returns go to investors who enter before the inflection. The inflection is anticipated by monitoring cost curves — as costs fall below critical thresholds, adoption accelerates.
- **Diversify Across Converging Platforms, Concentrate Within** *(ARK portfolio construction; ARK fund overlap analysis)* — Portfolio construction should diversify across multiple innovation platforms to reduce platform-specific risk while concentrating within platforms in the highest-conviction names. Owning the top 3 companies in each of the 5 platforms is a different proposition from owning 20 companies in each.
- **The Fear-Greed Positioning Counter-Signal** *(Cathie Wood on the 2022 drawdown; ARK response to market fear)* — When public sentiment is at maximum fear, the expected returns from conviction positions are highest. When sentiment is at maximum greed, expected returns are lowest. The correct reaction to fear is to prepare to buy; the correct reaction to greed is to prepare to trim.

## Layer 3 — Stopping Taste *(10 doctrines)*
*When to exit, when to hold through drawdowns, when to add.*

Wood's stopping doctrine makes a sharp distinction between price-driven exits (almost never correct for thesis-intact positions) and thesis-driven exits (immediately required when the thesis breaks). The 2022 drawdown tested this discipline severely — ARK held and even added to positions that had declined 70%+ because the innovation theses remained intact. The correct stopping criterion is never the price; it is always the thesis.

### From ARK exit philosophy and the 2022 drawdown record

- **Exit When the Technology Curve Changes** *(ARK exit philosophy; Wood on thesis-driven vs. price-driven exits)* — The correct exit trigger is a change in the technology's underlying cost curve — evidence that Wright's Law is not playing out as expected, that a superior technology has emerged, or that regulatory conditions have permanently closed the market. Price declines without these events do not justify exit.
- **Hold When Consensus Has Not Yet Caught Up** *(ARK holding philosophy; Cathie Wood on consensus convergence)* — The reason to hold a disruptive innovation position is the conviction that the consensus is wrong and will eventually catch up. As long as the market underappreciates the technology's trajectory, there is alpha in the position. Hold until the consensus catches up, then evaluate.
- **Stop When Superior Technology Emerges** *(ARK investment philosophy; disruptive innovation theory)* — When a superior technology makes the invested technology obsolete — and you believe this sincerely — exit immediately regardless of current price. The exit trigger is not competition between companies but competition between technologies.
- **Stop When the Regulatory Environment Has Permanently Closed** *(ARK regulatory risk framework; crypto regulatory monitoring)* — Regulation can permanently close a market opportunity, not just delay it. If regulatory action has structurally blocked the key innovation mechanism, the thesis may be broken regardless of the underlying technology's promise. When a thesis-breaking regulatory event occurs, exit promptly.
- **The Drawdown Discipline — Do Not Panic-Sell** *(ARK 2022 drawdown management; Cathie Wood on holding through drawdowns)* — A thesis-intact position that has declined 75% has higher expected returns than before the decline — if the thesis is truly intact. When a position has declined 30-50%, explicitly re-examine the thesis. Has the cost curve changed? Has adoption slowed beyond model? Has a superior technology emerged? If all three are no, the case for holding or adding is stronger.
- **Stop When Valuation Exceeds 5-Year DCF** *(ARK price target models; Tesla valuation evolution 2020-2022)* — When current market price has moved above the 5-year discounted cash flow estimate in the bull scenario, the expected return from the position has declined below the required threshold. Not a target price to celebrate — a target price to evaluate.
- **Trim for Risk Management, Not for Return Optimization** *(ARK portfolio construction; position sizing discipline)* — When a single position becomes an uncomfortably large percentage of the portfolio due to appreciation, trim to a manageable weight. This is risk management, not a view on the investment. Maintain the thesis while managing position sizing.
- **Exit Crowded Innovation Trades** *(ARK positioning analysis; Cathie Wood on differentiation)* — When every growth fund has the same disruptive innovation positions — when the constraint arbitrage has closed because the constraint no longer applies — re-evaluate whether the position still offers superior expected returns.
- **The Platform Supersession Signal** *(ARK platform evolution discussion; innovation theory)* — The five innovation platforms are themselves subject to supersession. When evidence emerges that a platform is being structurally displaced by something outside the framework — not just improved, but replaced — reduce exposure to that platform.
- **The Bitcoin Store-of-Value Thesis Test** *(ARK Bitcoin price target memos; ARK Big Ideas 2021-2025)* — ARK's Bitcoin thesis is specifically that it will function as a digital store of value and institutional reserve asset. The stopping criterion: evidence that this function is being replaced by an alternative, or that institutional adoption has peaked without reaching the expected ceiling.

## Verdict logic

Run each doctrine against the candidate technology investment. Mark **Pass / Weak / Fail** with notes. Then:

- **Kill** — any single Fail in Layer 1 (Problem-Selection), or 3+ Fails total
- **Proceed** — zero Fails, ≤2 Weaks, ≥60% Pass coverage across scored doctrines
- **Reconsider** — anything else

The Wright's Law doctrine is the master filter: if you cannot construct a cost curve projection that shows a meaningful cost decline over a 5-year production horizon, you may be looking at an incremental improvement rather than a disruptive innovation. The Wood doctrine applies to technologies following Wright's Law, not to standard competitive businesses.
