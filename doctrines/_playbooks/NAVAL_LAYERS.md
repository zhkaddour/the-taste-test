# The Naval Doctrine — Three Evaluation Layers (Deep Dive)

> *"Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is assets that earn while you sleep."*

A three-layer filter for building wealth and choosing work. Distilled from the 'How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)' tweetstorm (May 2018), 'The Almanack of Naval Ravikant' (Jorgenson, 2020), the Naval podcast corpus, Tim Ferriss interviews (#97, #250, #473), Joe Rogan #1309, and the full @naval Twitter corpus (2007–present).

**77 doctrines across 3 layers.** Every doctrine carries a *rule*, a concrete *test*, and its *source*.

---

## Layer 1 — Problem-Selection Taste *(32 doctrines)*
*What is worth working on at all.*

Naval's entire framework begins with one distinction: wealth versus money versus status. Most people confuse these three and optimize for the wrong one. Problem-selection means choosing work where you can build equity — ownership of something that generates value while you sleep — rather than renting out your time for a salary. The filter is applied before any tactics: wrong category of work = no amount of skill saves you.

### From *The Almanack* and the How to Get Rich tweetstorm

- **Wealth vs. Money vs. Status** *(How to Get Rich tweetstorm, May 2018)* — Wealth is assets that earn while you sleep; money is transfer; status is social rank. Most people play money or status games and wonder why they never build wealth.
- **Specific Knowledge First** *(How to Get Rich; Almanack)* — Specific knowledge is knowledge you cannot be trained for. It lives at the intersection of genuine curiosity, natural obsession, and things you learned before you knew you were learning.
- **Genuine Curiosity Beats Forced Study** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Curiosity is the most powerful learning accelerant. Specific knowledge must feel like play to you even if it looks like work to others.
- **Avoid Renting Out Your Time** *(How to Get Rich; Almanack)* — Any work where inputs and outputs are directly coupled is a treadmill with no exit. Wealth requires decoupling time from output.
- **Productize Yourself** *(How to Get Rich; Almanack; Tim Ferriss #473)* — What is the most scalable expression of you? Code, books, podcasts, courses — permissionless leverage that scales your specific knowledge to millions.
- **The Four Types of Leverage** *(How to Get Rich; Almanack)* — Labor, capital, code, media. Code and media are the new leverage — permissionless, free to replicate, available to anyone with a laptop.
- **Code and Media Are Permissionless** *(How to Get Rich; Almanack)* — Capital and labor require someone's permission. Code and media do not. Permissionless leverage is available to the intellectual, not only the connected.
- **Work With People Who Have All Three** *(Almanack; @naval)* — High intelligence, high energy, high integrity. All three are required. Any missing property makes the combination dangerous or useless.
- **Long-Term Games With Long-Term People** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Trust compounds. Reputation takes years to build and seconds to destroy. The best deals come to people who are trusted unconditionally.
- **The Judgment Leverage** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — With infinite leverage, judgment becomes the binding constraint. Cultivate and sell your judgment — the higher the leverage, the more valuable a good decision.
- **Societal Leverage via Technology** *(Naval podcast; Almanack; Tim Ferriss #97)* — Every software product, every published idea that gets adopted is society deploying your work without your involvement.
- **The Best Business = High Margins + Scalable** *(Naval podcast)* — Software businesses are the canonical example. Low margins that scale with revenue are structurally incapable of building wealth.
- **Work for Equity, Not Salary** *(How to Get Rich; Almanack)* — Salary is renting your time. Equity is ownership of a machine that runs without you.
- **Accountability Unlocks Leverage** *(How to Get Rich; Almanack)* — Taking ownership under your own name is what unlocks the ability to accumulate wealth and reputation.
- **Find the Intersection of Three** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — The ideal work sits where: what you're good at, what you enjoy, and what the world pays for all overlap. Any two of three is insufficient.
- **Avoid Zero-Sum Status Games** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Status games are zero-sum. Wealth games are positive-sum. People playing status games try to tear others down.
- **Build in the New World, Not the Old** *(Naval podcast)* — The old world required capital, connections, credentials, geography. The new world requires internet access and specific knowledge.
- **The Retirement Frame** *(Almanack; Farnam Street)* — Retirement is not stopping work. It is reaching a state where you do what you want, when you want, with whom you want.
- **Pick the Game You Want to Play, Then Learn the Rules** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Most people play games they fell into. Explicitly choose the game. The right game, played with specific knowledge and leverage, compounds for decades.
- **The Internet Enables Global-Scale Niches** *(Naval podcast; @naval Twitter)* — Before the internet, being the world's best at something very specific was economically worthless. Now extreme specificity is a business model.
- **Don't Partner With Cynics or Pessimists** *(Almanack; @naval)* — Their beliefs become self-fulfilling. Pessimists systematically underinvest in building.
- **The Darwinian Filter of Starting a Company** *(Almanack)* — Starting a company is the most direct test of specific knowledge, leverage, accountability, and judgment.
- **Signals of Specific Knowledge** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Three signals: you teach yourself better than any class could, people ask your opinion unsolicited, you lose track of time doing it.
- **Build Distribution Early** *(Naval podcast; AngelList)* — Distribution is leverage. An audience is an asset that amplifies every future product you make.
- **The Compounding Horizon Must Be Long** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Compounding works best over decades, not years. Choose a domain you can compound in for 20 years.
- **Learn to Sell and Learn to Build** *(tweetstorm; Almanack)* — If you can do both, you will be unstoppable. These are the only two skills that matter for a founder.
- **Ignore Salary-Capped Paths Early** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Any career with a hard salary ceiling is structurally limited. The best early careers are fat-tailed.
- **The Specific Knowledge Stack** *(Almanack; Tim Ferriss #250)* — Specific knowledge can be stacked. The combination of two rare skills creates a unique position nobody else occupies.
- **The Path of No Regrets** *(Almanack)* — Regrets of action fade; regrets of inaction compound. At age 80 the calculus is clear. Take the other path.
- **Be a Free Agent, Not a Dependent** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Have so much specific knowledge and clear value that you can leave anyone and the market absorbs you immediately.
- **Pick Honesty as a Business Strategy** *(Almanack; @naval)* — Every lie is debt repaid with interest. Be known as someone who tells hard truths diplomatically.
- **Escape Competition Through Authenticity** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — You can't be copied exactly. The more authentically you operate, the more you escape competition entirely.

## Layer 2 — Approach Taste *(28 doctrines)*
*Given the problem, which angle to attack from.*

Naval's approach doctrine is organized around one meta-principle: compounding. Every tactic is evaluated by whether it compounds — skills, relationships, reputation, knowledge. Tactics that don't compound (credential-chasing, status-seeking, busyness) are explicitly deprioritized regardless of their short-term appeal.

### Mindset and mental operating system

- **Read What You Love Until You Love to Read** *(Almanack; @naval)* — Start with what genuinely interests you. Volume requires desire. 500 books in your domain of obsession beats 50 important books you didn't want to read.
- **Learn How to Learn** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — The meta-skill of learning — how your brain retains information, what study methods work — multiplies every other skill you develop.
- **The Clear Mind Prerequisite** *(Almanack; Tim Ferriss #473)* — Good judgment requires a clear mind. Meditation, reduced news consumption, and controlled inputs are prerequisites for clear thinking.
- **Prioritize Health, Then Wealth, Then Love** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Health is the foundation. The order matters: most people build in the wrong sequence.
- **Meditation as Cognitive Maintenance** *(Almanack; Tim Ferriss #473)* — Meditation improves decision quality by reducing reactive thinking and increasing the space between stimulus and response.
- **Judgment Over Effort** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Work hard enough to stay in the game, but judgment — the quality of your decisions — matters more than quantity of effort.
- **Use Mental Models Relentlessly** *(Almanack; Farnam Street; Naval podcast)* — The breadth of your mental model library determines the quality of your cross-domain reasoning.
- **First Principles Over Analogy** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Reasoning by analogy carries all the assumptions of the comparison case. First principles asks what must be true.
- **Network Selectively and Deeply** *(Almanack; @naval)* — Networking for truth — finding people who think clearly and will be honest — is invaluable. Networking for access is largely a waste.
- **Build Products, Not Services** *(Almanack)* — Services scale with your time. Products scale without it. Same effort, one product serves 100,000 people as well as one.
- **Say No to Almost Everything** *(Almanack; @naval)* — Every commitment crowds out the possibility of a better commitment. If not enthusiastically yes, it's a no.
- **The 40-Hour Week Is an Industrial Artifact** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Knowledge work compounds unevenly. One week of deep insight can be worth 10 weeks of effort. Manage output, not time.
- **Micro-Decisions Compound** *(Almanack; @naval)* — How you do anything is how you do everything. Small recurring decisions define the habits and reputation that compound into large outcomes.
- **Become the Best in the World at What You Do** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Not top 1% — genuinely the world's best at a specific combination of skills. The internet connects you to everyone who needs exactly that.
- **The Honest Brag** *(Almanack; Naval podcast; AngelList)* — Sharing knowledge and results publicly is market signal. Silence about valuable work is a market inefficiency.
- **Play Iterated Games** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — In iterated games, cooperation pays. Structure professional life so all important relationships are iterated. Reputation is the shadow of iterated games.
- **Embrace Intellectual Curiosity as an Asset** *(Almanack; Tim Ferriss #97)* — Curious people ask better questions, learn faster, and spot opportunities earlier. Cultivate it deliberately.
- **Block Time for Thinking** *(Almanack)* — Protect large blocks of unscheduled time specifically for thinking — not reading, not outputting. Just thinking.
- **Write to Think** *(Almanack; @naval)* — Writing forces clarity. The act of explaining an idea exposes logical gaps and hidden assumptions that feel resolved in your head.
- **The Single Virtue of Good Advisors** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — A good advisor tells you what you don't want to hear. The one who makes you feel good is providing comfort, not advice.
- **Operate at Your Unique Energy Peak** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Know your two best cognitive hours per day. Protect them for your most important work.
- **The Two-Minute Rule for Small Decisions** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Small decisions receive the same deliberation as large ones, which is cognitively wasteful. Decide fast on low stakes; spend time on high stakes.
- **Environment Design Beats Willpower** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Design your environment so the right behaviors are default. Structure beats discipline.
- **The Short-Cut Versus Long-Cut Frame** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — There is no shortcut to building genuine specific knowledge. The 'shortcuts' are the long cuts. The overnight success took 20 years.
- **Copy From Nature, Not From People** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — The best mental models come from physics, biology, mathematics. Nature's solutions are optimal; human solutions are suboptimal at best.
- **Choose Wealth-Building Over Signaling** *(Almanack)* — Conspicuous consumption produces no wealth. The wealthiest people Naval knows often live simply.
- **Trust the Process, Not the Prediction** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — You cannot predict which specific bets pay off, but a good process — working with high-integrity people, building specific knowledge, deploying leverage — produces wealth over time.
- **Be Impatient With Actions, Patient With Results** *(Almanack; @naval)* — Act fast on decisions and experiments. Be patient with the compounding that follows. Most people have this exactly backward.

## Layer 3 — Stopping Taste *(17 doctrines)*
*When to abandon, when to push, when done enough.*

Naval's stopping doctrine is surprisingly clear-cut: stop when you've lost genuine interest, when the game is zero-sum, when the environment cannot support your growth, or when the compounding has stopped. Hold when genuine compounding continues.

### Exit signals

- **Boredom Is a Signal, Not a Problem** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Boredom means the compounding has stopped. It is information about fit, not a motivation problem to overcome with discipline.
- **If It's Not Hell Yes, It's No** *(Almanack; @naval)* — For major commitments, if your response is not an unambiguous yes, it should be a no. 'Pretty good' crowds out great.
- **Exit Toxic Environments Immediately** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Environments that reward political behavior, punish honesty, or prevent the accumulation of specific knowledge cannot be fixed from inside. Exit.
- **Stop Optimizing for Appearance** *(Almanack; @naval)* — When you catch yourself making decisions to look good rather than produce results, stop. The status game has infiltrated the wealth game.
- **The Sunk Cost Exit Rule** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Time, money, reputation already spent cannot be recovered. The right question is always: given everything I know now, would I start this today?
- **The Addiction Test** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — For any habit, platform, or activity: can you put it down for a week without anxiety? If not, you have lost freedom of choice.
- **Hold When Compounding Is Real** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Genuine compounding is often invisible short-term and dramatic long-term. Don't confuse slow visible progress with no progress.
- **The Path Dependency Trap** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Most people are on paths they chose years ago with less information. Periodically re-evaluate from first principles: is this the best allocation of your one life?
- **Stop Status Games When You Catch Yourself Playing Them** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Status games are recognizable: comparing to peers, competitive rather than curious, decisions based on rank. Notice and stop.
- **"I Don't Have Time" = "It's Not a Priority"** *(Almanack; @naval)* — You have the same 168 hours as everyone else. 'No time' is a values statement in disguise. Acknowledge the true reason.
- **The "Would I Hire Myself?" Test** *(Almanack; AngelList)* — For every role you fill: would you hire the person doing it? Apply this to yourself. Wrong role = move to one where you are the clear best person.
- **Stop When Your Highest Leverage Isn't Here** *(Almanack; tweetstorm)* — Where is the highest-leverage use of your specific knowledge right now? If not where you are, that's your exit signal.
- **Enough Is a Personal Definition** *(Almanack; Joe Rogan #1309)* — Define what enough looks like financially before you reach it. People who don't define enough never reach it.
- **The One-Year Test for Regret** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — In one year, will you regret having left, or regret having stayed? The regret asymmetry is real.
- **Rebuild After Failure Immediately** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — Motion beats reflection in the aftermath of setbacks. The longer you wait after failure before the next bet, the harder it becomes.
- **The Permissionless Exit** *(Almanack; @naval)* — You don't need anyone's permission to stop. Exit social obligations and status-driven commitments without guilt.
- **Choose Association Deliberately** *(Almanack; Naval podcast)* — You are the average of the people you spend the most time with. Choose intentionally; exit gracefully when the fit is wrong.

## 2026 Overlay

### Where the alpha is
- AI-enabled specific knowledge businesses: individuals with deep domain expertise + AI leverage = individual unicorns
- Permissionless media at scale: the individual brand is now more valuable than the institutional brand in many verticals
- Crypto/decentralized finance: permissionless wealth-building mechanisms that disintermediate traditional gatekeepers
- Physical world applications of software leverage: robotics, bio, energy — sectors where software leverage is newly applicable
- Individual-to-individual trust networks replacing institutional trust as AI makes institutional trust more fragile

### Auto-reject
- Credential-optimizing career paths where the credential is the moat
- Any business where the primary leverage is regulatory capture
- Zero-sum competitive markets where winning requires someone else to lose rather than new value created
- High-status, low-wealth positions (fancy job titles at large organizations without equity)
- Work that requires permanent busyness rather than deep thinking and judgment

## Verdict logic

Run each doctrine against the candidate idea or career decision. Mark **Pass / Weak / Fail** with notes. Then:

- **Kill** — any single Fail in Layer 1 (Problem-Selection), or 3+ Fails total
- **Proceed** — zero Fails, ≤2 Weaks, ≥60% Pass coverage across scored doctrines
- **Reconsider** — anything else

The Wealth vs. Status distinction sits at the top of Layer 1 as the primary filter: if you're building in a status game rather than a wealth game, every subsequent doctrine is answering the wrong question.
