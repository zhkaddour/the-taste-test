## Layer 1 — Problem-Selection Taste
*Where to invest — finding transformative innovation*

Cathie Wood's investment doctrine is built on a single insight: genuinely transformative technologies follow predictable S-curve adoption patterns with dramatically falling costs. The investor who recognizes these technologies before the S-curve inflects — when the technology is still in the disbelief or skepticism phase — earns returns that are structurally unavailable to the consensus investor. The filter for where to invest is not 'what is currently popular' but 'what technology will seem obvious in 10 years that looks premature today?'

### The Five Innovation Platforms
**Rule:** The transformative technologies of the current era are organized around five platforms: (1) DNA sequencing and genomics, (2) robotics and autonomous systems, (3) energy storage and EVs, (4) artificial intelligence, (5) blockchain/cryptocurrency. All other technology innovation is either a subset of these or does not meet the transformative threshold.

**Test:** Which of the five platforms does this investment sit within? If it cannot be mapped to one of the five, it may be incremental innovation rather than transformative. Require explicit platform mapping before investing.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas 2017-2025; ARK investment philosophy white papers

### Disruptive Innovation Definition
**Rule:** Disruptive innovation meets four criteria: (1) enabled by technology, not just business model innovation; (2) crosses major adoption curves from early adopter to mainstream; (3) dramatic cost declines over time via Wright's Law dynamics; (4) solves previously unsolvable problems. All four must be present.

**Test:** Score the investment on all four criteria: (1) Is the advantage technology-enabled or business-model only? (2) Is there a clear adoption curve to mainstream? (3) Are unit costs declining on a Wright's Law trajectory? (4) Does it solve something previously unsolvable? Any missing criterion disqualifies the "disruptive" label.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas 2017; ARK investment philosophy; Lex Fridman interview 2021

### Wright's Law — The Cost Curve Is the Thesis
**Rule:** Wright's Law: for every cumulative doubling of production, unit costs fall by a consistent percentage. EVs, solar panels, DNA sequencing, and battery storage all follow Wright's Law trajectories. The investment thesis is not primarily about current economics — it is about where the cost curve will be in 5 years.

**Test:** Fit a Wright's Law curve to the last 10 years of production volume vs. unit cost data. What does the curve project for cost in 5 years given plausible production growth? If the 5-year projected cost makes the technology competitive with incumbents, the thesis is alive.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas 2017-2024; ARK Tesla model methodology

### Consensus Is Priced In
**Rule:** The investor whose thesis matches the consensus earns consensus returns — which are average by definition. Alpha requires a thesis that diverges from consensus in a direction that turns out to be correct.

**Test:** State the consensus view on this investment in one sentence. Does the current thesis match or diverge from it? If they match, the opportunity has closed. Only thesis divergence from consensus produces alpha.

**Source:** ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood interviews 2020-2024

### The 5-Year Horizon Minimum
**Rule:** Disruptive innovation takes 5-10 years to move from early to mainstream adoption. Quarterly earnings reports, short-term revenue growth, and near-term profitability are poor signals for transformative companies.

**Test:** Is the investment decision justified by 5-year fundamentals or by next-quarter metrics? If the thesis breaks down on a 12-month horizon, it is not a disruptive innovation investment — it is a momentum trade.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas annual reports; Cathie Wood interviews; Lex Fridman 2021

### Platform Convergence Multiplies Returns
**Rule:** The largest opportunities arise where two or more of the five innovation platforms converge. AI + genomics = drug discovery at unprecedented speed. AI + robotics = physical world automation. Each convergence creates a market that no single platform could create alone.

**Test:** Does this investment sit at the convergence of two or more platforms? Convergence investments should be weighted more heavily because the addressable market is the product of both platform TAMs, not just one.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas 2022-2025; ARK convergence thesis documents

### Invest in the Disbelief and Skepticism Phases
**Rule:** Technology adoption follows an emotional arc: disbelief → skepticism → acceptance → enthusiasm. The largest returns accrue to investors who enter in the disbelief or skepticism phase.

**Test:** Map the technology's current emotional adoption phase. Is the dominant institutional posture disbelief or skepticism? If it is acceptance or enthusiasm, entry is too late for maximum returns.

**Source:** ARK investment philosophy; Gartner Hype Cycle analogy; Cathie Wood on market psychology

### Category Creation vs. Category Competition
**Rule:** The transformative companies create categories that don't yet exist. Tesla didn't compete in the automobile market — it created the EV market. Category creators earn structurally better economics than category competitors.

**Test:** Is this company competing in an existing category, or defining a new one? If a journalist would describe it in the same sentence as an existing incumbent, it is competing, not creating. Category creators are described with new vocabulary.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas; Tesla investment thesis evolution 2014-2025

### The Market Doesn't Understand Yet Arbitrage
**Rule:** The most reliable source of alpha is the window of time between when a technology becomes genuinely promising and when the broad institutional market recognizes it. Career risk prevents most institutional investors from participating — which keeps prices low.

**Test:** What specific institutional constraint prevents the broad market from currently holding this position? Identify the constraint (benchmark tracking, rating mandates, career risk, liquidity requirements). No identifiable constraint = the arbitrage window may be closed.

**Source:** Cathie Wood interviews on institutional constraints; ARK investment philosophy

### The Open Research Model as Competitive Advantage
**Rule:** Publishing research, models, and theses publicly is not charity — it is a competitive strategy. Publishing attracts the best analysts and guest contributors, builds trust with investors, and forces rigor.

**Test:** Is the investment thesis robust enough to publish publicly? If the thesis cannot withstand public scrutiny, it may contain errors that private opacity is hiding. Public models improve through feedback; private models accumulate uncorrected bias.

**Source:** ARK open research model; Cathie Wood on transparency as strategy

### The Innovation Lifecycle: From Disbelief to Bubble to Real Value
**Rule:** Transformative technologies often go through a bubble phase before reaching the phase where real value is created. The dot-com bubble was a bubble; the internet was not a failed technology. Distinguish the bubble from the underlying technology.

**Test:** Has this technology gone through a bubble phase? If yes, is the current price reflecting post-bubble real fundamentals or bubble-era speculation? Post-bubble entry on a technology with proven fundamentals is a high-value entry point.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas 2021-2023; Cathie Wood on the crypto bear market; dot-com analogy

### The Institutional Constraint Arbitrage
**Rule:** Most institutional investors face constraints ARK does not: benchmark tracking, rating-agency-driven mandates, quarterly redemption risk, career risk. These constraints systematically exclude the best early-stage disruptive investments, creating a structural pricing opportunity.

**Test:** For this specific investment, which institutional constraint is keeping the price lower than fundamentals warrant? If no constraint can be named, the pricing opportunity may not be structural.

**Source:** ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood on institutional constraints

### The 'What Does AI Unlock Here?' Question
**Rule:** Artificial intelligence is a general-purpose technology that will materially accelerate progress in every other innovation platform. The question for any domain: what problems are currently gated by the speed of human cognition or the cost of analysis, and how does AI change that gate?

**Test:** Name the specific bottleneck in this domain that AI eliminates. If AI makes everything incrementally better but removes no specific bottleneck, the AI tailwind is a headwind in disguise — it may commoditize the existing solution rather than create a new category.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas 2023-2025; Cathie Wood on AI convergence with other platforms

## Layer 2 — Approach Taste
*How to invest — conviction, analysis, and portfolio construction*

ARK's investment approach is built around three non-standard convictions: (1) the correct benchmark is absolute return over a 5-year horizon, not relative return over a quarter; (2) conviction must be built through independent analysis, not synthesized from consensus; (3) the correct response to a thesis-affirming price decline is to buy more, not to exit. Each of these convictions contradicts mainstream institutional practice — which is why they can be a source of alpha.

### The Three-Scenario Model
**Rule:** For every major position, build a bull case, base case, and bear case price target — each with explicit assumptions about technology adoption, cost curves, and market penetration. The expected value is probability-weighted across scenarios.

**Test:** For the current position, are all three scenarios explicitly modeled with specific assumptions? If only a base case exists, the upside and downside are unmeasured. Require explicit probability-weighted scenario modeling.

**Source:** ARK Tesla price target models; ARK Bitcoin price target memos; ARK research methodology

### Conviction Holding — Add on Thesis-Affirming Declines
**Rule:** When a stock falls because the market doesn't understand the innovation thesis — not because the thesis has changed — the correct response is to increase the position size.

**Test:** For a declining position, answer two questions: (1) Has the technology cost curve changed? (2) Has the adoption trajectory changed? If both are no, the thesis is intact and the decline is a buying opportunity.

**Source:** ARK 2022 drawdown and response; Cathie Wood on conviction investing; Lex Fridman 2021

### Reject Benchmark-Relative Thinking
**Rule:** Benchmark-relative investors ask 'how much did we underperform the S&P this quarter?' Absolute-return investors ask 'what will this position be worth in 5 years?' The benchmark frame will systematically cause you to exit positions that are tracking your 5-year thesis.

**Test:** Is each portfolio decision evaluated against the 5-year thesis or against last quarter's index performance? If the portfolio is trimmed because of short-term underperformance against an index, benchmark thinking has infected the decision.

**Source:** ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood on benchmark risk

### Interview the Inventors, Not the Executives
**Rule:** The most reliable information about a technology's potential comes from the people doing the research — not from executives, investor relations teams, or sell-side analysts.

**Test:** For the current investment, has the analyst interviewed the researchers (not the executives or IR) who are doing the actual technical work? Executive information is filtered by incentives. Researcher information is filtered by reality.

**Source:** ARK research process; Cathie Wood on expert network as research edge

### The Cost Curve Projection Method
**Rule:** The core analytical tool: identify the relevant technology's unit cost over the last 10+ years, fit a Wright's Law curve to the data, project forward using volume assumptions. The projected cost in Year 5 determines what markets become addressable.

**Test:** Has a Wright's Law curve been fitted to at least 10 years of production volume and unit cost data? Without this fitting, the cost thesis is assertion rather than analysis.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas methodology; ARK Tesla battery model; ARK solar model

### The 'Disbelief, Skepticism, Acceptance, Enthusiasm' Timing Framework
**Rule:** ARK's entry timing maps to the emotional adoption cycle. The sweet spot is early in skepticism: enough evidence exists that the technology is real, but not enough for institutional investors to participate.

**Test:** What is the dominant institutional posture toward this technology today? Disbelief: too early (no evidence yet). Skepticism: optimal entry (evidence exists, institutions haven't committed). Acceptance: late entry. Enthusiasm: exit signal.

**Source:** ARK investment philosophy; Cathie Wood on market psychology

### Concentration in the Highest-Conviction Positions
**Rule:** ARK runs concentrated portfolios by design. Diversification for its own sake dilutes the returns from the highest-conviction positions.

**Test:** Does the portfolio reflect actual conviction hierarchy? The top 5 positions should represent significantly more capital than positions 6-20. If the portfolio is equally weighted across 30+ positions, diversification has diluted conviction.

**Source:** ARK portfolio construction; Cathie Wood on concentration vs. diversification

### Public Models as Intellectual Honesty
**Rule:** By publishing price target models publicly, ARK creates accountability for its analysis. Public models can be criticized, improved, and proven wrong.

**Test:** Is the price target model public, with explicit assumptions that can be challenged? If the model is proprietary, the assumptions cannot be tested by critics and will accumulate unchallenged bias.

**Source:** ARK Tesla price target publications; ARK Bitcoin price targets; ARK research methodology

### Price Targets Are 5-Year Present Values
**Rule:** ARK's price targets are 5-year discounted cash flow estimates, not next-quarter consensus price targets. A stock can be at $200 today and have a 5-year price target of $1,000 — the intervening path matters less than the destination.

**Test:** Is the price target derived from 5-year DCF analysis or from near-term multiples? Near-term multiple expansion targets are not disruptive innovation analysis.

**Source:** ARK price target methodology; Tesla, Bitcoin, and Zoom models

### The Open Ecosystem of Research Contributors
**Rule:** ARK's open research platform — where domain experts can contribute analysis publicly — creates an ecosystem of intelligence that a closed research team cannot replicate.

**Test:** Are outside domain experts regularly contributing to and challenging the investment thesis? A closed-team thesis is more prone to confirmation bias than one regularly challenged by domain experts.

**Source:** ARK open research platform; ARK guest contributor model

### The S-Curve Entry Point
**Rule:** Technology adoption follows an S-curve: slow initial adoption, then an inflection to rapid growth, then maturation. The highest returns go to investors who enter before the inflection.

**Test:** Where is the current technology on the S-curve? Plot cumulative adoption vs. time. Is the curve still in the slow-growth pre-inflection phase? If post-inflection, much of the return has already been realized.

**Source:** ARK Big Ideas; Wright's Law and S-curve relationship; ARK adoption curve analysis

### Diversify Across Converging Platforms, Concentrate Within
**Rule:** Portfolio construction should diversify across multiple innovation platforms to reduce platform-specific risk while concentrating within platforms in the highest-conviction names.

**Test:** Does the portfolio have exposure across at least 3 of the 5 innovation platforms? And within each platform, is capital concentrated in the 2-3 highest-conviction names rather than spread evenly? Both conditions required.

**Source:** ARK portfolio construction; ARK fund overlap analysis

### The Fear-Greed Positioning Counter-Signal
**Rule:** When public sentiment is at maximum fear, the expected returns from conviction positions are highest. When sentiment is at maximum greed, expected returns are lowest.

**Test:** What is the current sentiment signal for this technology (CNN Fear & Greed Index, fund flows, media tone)? Maximum fear = prepare to buy or add. Maximum greed = prepare to trim. Act against the sentiment signal, not with it.

**Source:** Cathie Wood on the 2022 drawdown; ARK response to market fear

## Layer 3 — Stopping Taste
*When to exit, when to hold through drawdowns, when to add*

Wood's stopping doctrine makes a sharp distinction between price-driven exits (almost never correct for thesis-intact positions) and thesis-driven exits (immediately required when the thesis breaks). The 2022 drawdown tested this discipline severely — ARK held and even added to positions that had declined 70%+ because the innovation theses remained intact. The correct stopping criterion is never the price; it is always the thesis.

### Exit When the Technology Curve Changes
**Rule:** The correct exit trigger is a change in the technology's underlying cost curve — evidence that Wright's Law is not playing out as expected, that a superior technology has emerged, or that regulatory conditions have permanently closed the market.

**Test:** Has the Wright's Law cost curve flattened or reversed relative to the projection? If unit costs are not declining at the projected rate, the fundamental thesis is breaking down and exit should be considered.

**Source:** ARK exit philosophy; Wood on thesis-driven vs. price-driven exits

### Hold When Consensus Has Not Yet Caught Up
**Rule:** The reason to hold a disruptive innovation position is the conviction that the consensus is wrong and will eventually catch up. Hold until the consensus catches up, then evaluate.

**Test:** Has the consensus caught up to the original thesis? Check sell-side price targets and institutional ownership. If both have moved to the original thesis, the alpha has been harvested and the hold case has weakened.

**Source:** ARK holding philosophy; Cathie Wood on consensus convergence

### Stop When Superior Technology Emerges
**Rule:** When a superior technology makes the invested technology obsolete — and you believe this sincerely — exit immediately regardless of current price.

**Test:** Has a technology emerged that is on a materially better cost curve on the same performance dimension? If yes, the invested technology's adoption S-curve will be interrupted. Exit and redirect to the superior technology.

**Source:** ARK investment philosophy; disruptive innovation theory

### Stop When the Regulatory Environment Has Permanently Closed
**Rule:** Regulation can permanently close a market opportunity, not just delay it. If regulatory action has structurally blocked the key innovation mechanism, the thesis may be broken regardless of the underlying technology's promise.

**Test:** Has a regulatory action permanently closed the innovation mechanism — not delayed it, but structurally prevented the cost-curve adoption pattern from playing out? Temporary restriction is not a thesis-break; permanent closure is.

**Source:** ARK regulatory risk framework; crypto regulatory monitoring

### The Drawdown Discipline — Do Not Panic-Sell
**Rule:** A thesis-intact position that has declined 75% has higher expected returns than before the decline — if the thesis is truly intact. When a position has declined 30-50%, explicitly re-examine the thesis.

**Test:** Run the three-question thesis check: (1) Has the cost curve changed? (2) Has adoption slowed beyond model? (3) Has a superior technology emerged? If all three are no, the expected return has increased on the decline. Hold or add.

**Source:** ARK 2022 drawdown management; Cathie Wood on holding through drawdowns

### Stop When Valuation Exceeds 5-Year DCF
**Rule:** When current market price has moved above the 5-year discounted cash flow estimate in the bull scenario, the expected return from the position has declined below the required threshold.

**Test:** Compare the current price to the 5-year bull-case DCF estimate. If current price > bull-case 5-year target, the expected return has fallen below the minimum threshold even in the optimistic scenario. This is an exit signal, not a celebration.

**Source:** ARK price target models; Tesla valuation evolution 2020-2022

### Trim for Risk Management, Not for Return Optimization
**Rule:** When a single position becomes an uncomfortably large percentage of the portfolio due to appreciation, trim to a manageable weight. This is risk management, not a view on the investment.

**Test:** Has any single position exceeded 15% of the portfolio due to appreciation? If yes, trim to 10-12% as a position sizing discipline, not because the thesis has changed. Distinguish size-driven trimming from thesis-driven selling.

**Source:** ARK portfolio construction; position sizing discipline

### Exit Crowded Innovation Trades
**Rule:** When every growth fund has the same disruptive innovation positions — when the constraint arbitrage has closed because the constraint no longer applies — re-evaluate whether the position still offers superior expected returns.

**Test:** Check the top-10 holdings of the 10 largest growth funds. Is this position appearing in >5 of them? If so, the institutional constraint arbitrage has closed and the position is now consensus. The alpha window has likely closed.

**Source:** ARK positioning analysis; Cathie Wood on differentiation

### The Platform Supersession Signal
**Rule:** The five innovation platforms are themselves subject to supersession. When evidence emerges that a platform is being structurally displaced by something outside the framework — not just improved, but replaced — reduce exposure to that platform.

**Test:** Is there credible evidence that one of the five platforms is being displaced by a fundamentally different approach rather than incrementally improved? Platform supersession is different from within-platform competition. Require evidence, not speculation.

**Source:** ARK platform evolution discussion; innovation theory

### The Bitcoin Store-of-Value Thesis Test
**Rule:** ARK's Bitcoin thesis is specifically that it will function as a digital store of value and institutional reserve asset. The stopping criterion: evidence that this function is being replaced by an alternative, or that institutional adoption has peaked without reaching the expected ceiling.

**Test:** Track two metrics: (1) institutional Bitcoin holdings as a % of total assets under management and (2) competitor digital assets taking share of the store-of-value function. If institutional adoption has stalled far below the projected ceiling, the thesis warrants re-examination.

**Source:** ARK Bitcoin price target memos; ARK Big Ideas 2021-2025
